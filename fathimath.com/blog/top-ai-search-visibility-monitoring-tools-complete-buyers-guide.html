How AI visibility tools work

An AI visibility tool runs a fixed set of prompts across multiple answer engines on a schedule, records how each engine mentions, cites, ranks, and describes your brand, and turns those gaps into an optimization plan. Under the hood, four things happen:

  1. Prompting. The platform runs a curated prompt set (branded, category, and comparison queries) across engines — either by scraping the real chat UIs (closer to what a user sees) or calling model APIs (cheaper, less accurate).
  2. Observation. It logs mentions, citations (the exact source pages), position, sentiment, and share of AI voice, then benchmarks you against named competitors.
  3. Action. The better tools don't stop at dashboards — they surface prioritized fixes: semantic content edits, schema, llms.txt/robots checks, and outreach targets.
  4. Attribution. The best tools connect AI impressions to sessions and revenue via GA4, so AEO stops being a science project.

One caveat that applies to every tool on this list: AI answers are volatile, and the sources they cite shift fast. When Goodie analyzed 58.6 million AI citations across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity, 13 of the 20 most-cited domains had turned over versus its prior study — the biggest shift the team had recorded. And a Semrush three-month study watched ChatGPT's Reddit citations fall from roughly 60% of responses in early August to about 10% by mid-September 2025. Treat any single snapshot as directional and track trends over time.

The scoring model I used

I created an AI Search Visibility Optimization Score (ASVO) out of 100, weighted for what actually moves the needle in 2026:

  1. LLM & Surface Coverage (15%) – ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, DeepSeek, Grok, Meta AI, Copilot, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Amazon Rufus/Alexa for Shopping, etc.
  2. Observability Depth (20%) – mentions/share-of-voice, exact citations and source mapping, sentiment/position, refresh cadence, multi-country/language.
  3. Action Layer (15%) – agentic workflows to fix gaps (semantic edits, schema, robots/llms.txt checks, outreach briefs), and content recommendations designed for citability.
  4. Technical AEO (10%) – agent/crawler analytics, log integrations, structured-data audits, AIO/GEO diagnostics.
  5. AI Shopping (10%) – monitoring/optimization for ChatGPT / Perplexity / Amazon surfaces.
  6. Analytics & Attribution (10%) – from LLM impressions to sessions, pipeline, and revenue.
  7. Enterprise Readiness (10%) – SSO, SOC 2, permissions, APIs, SLAs, regions/languages.
  8. Pricing Transparency & Accessibility (5%) – public pricing, trials, time-to-value.
  9. Education & Research (5%) – credible guides/research that help teams adopt AEO/GEO.

Method: scores are derived from public feature pages, pricing, docs, and enterprise claims. Where vendors publish hard compliance claims (e.g., SOC 2), I award full credit. Where detail is missing, I score conservatively.

A note on AI shopping (and why it's in flux)

  • ChatGPT: OpenAI launched Instant Checkout in late 2025 (Etsy first, Shopify next) but wound the in-chat checkout down in early 2026 after low merchant adoption, pivoting to a discovery-first model that routes shoppers to merchant apps/stores; the underlying Agentic Commerce Protocol (built with Stripe) survives.
  • Amazon: Renamed Rufus "Alexa for Shopping" on May 13, 2026 and shipped agentic buying (Auto-Buy, Buy for Me) — while fencing its store off from third-party agents and suing Perplexity over its Comet browser.
  • Perplexity & Copilot: Perplexity added shopping/checkout; Microsoft launched Copilot Checkout at NRF 2026.
  • Google: AI Mode/AI Overviews keep expanding into shopping via the Universal Commerce Protocol with Walmart, Target, Shopify, Etsy, and others.

Bottom line: the surfaces are real but the mechanics are moving monthly. If your AEO tool can't see these surfaces, it can't guide your revenue strategy. For the tactical version, read my Practical AI Shopping AEO/GEO Guide.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between SEO and AEO — and will AEO replace SEO?
SEO helps people find you; AEO makes your brand the answer — and no, AEO won't fully replace SEO, it sits on top of it. The content signals that win AI citations (factual density, clear entities, structure) also build the E-E-A-T that traditional search rewards, so most teams run one program that serves both retrieval systems.
How much do AI visibility tools cost?
Entry pricing runs from about $50–$95/month for lightweight trackers to $399/month for full closed-loop platforms, with enterprise deals reaching four to five figures monthly. Concretely: ZipTie from $69/mo, Peec from $95/mo, HubSpot AEO ~$50/mo, Scrunch from $250/mo, Goodie Core $399/mo self-serve (annual), Profound self-serve from $99/mo (annual); Botify, Bluefish, and Evertune are custom/enterprise.
Which AI engines should a monitoring tool cover?
At minimum ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity — because they carry the majority of AI search volume — plus Google AI Mode, Gemini, Copilot, and Claude if those are where your buyers are. The engines barely overlap in what they cite, so single-engine tracking gives a false sense of completeness.
Can these tools track exact AI rankings?
No — AI answers are probabilistic, not a fixed ranked list, so tools report visibility, share of voice, and citation frequency across many runs, not a stable "position #1." Cited domains can turn over heavily between studies, which is why credible tools sample repeatedly and report trends.
Are there free AI search monitoring tools?
Yes, but they're one-time or limited snapshots, not ongoing programs — HubSpot's AEO Grader gives a free one-off check, and several vendors offer free audits or trials. Use them to baseline, then move to a paid tracker if AEO is a real channel for you.
What is share of AI voice?
Share of AI voice is the percentage of AI-generated answers in your category that mention your brand versus competitors — the AI-era equivalent of share of voice. Run a fixed prompt set across your target engines, count how many responses cite you, divide by the total, and track it over time and by engine.
Do we really need to monitor Google's AI experiences separately?
Yes — AI Overviews and AI Mode are distinct experiences with different triggers and layouts, and many vendors track them separately. If your customers use Google, both belong in your prompt set.
Can any vendor guarantee we "rank" in AI answers?
No — tools help the process, but none control the model. Your best odds come from being observable across surfaces, fixing the right content/technical issues fast, earning citations from trusted sources, and measuring outcomes so you can iterate.
