Most AI-visibility tools hand you a dashboard and stop there. Goodie doesn't, and I'd know, because I built it.

Full disclosure up front: I'm the founder and CEO of Goodie AI, so read this as a founder's honest teardown of his own product, not a pretend-neutral review. I'll tell you what it does well, where it falls short, and who should skip it.

What is Goodie AI? Goodie is an answer engine optimization (AEO) platform that monitors, diagnoses, and improves how a brand appears in AI search. It tracks visibility across up to 12 AI engines, surfaces the prompts and content gaps that matter, and helps ship the fixes, from content to schema to product feeds.

Is it worth it? If AI search is a channel you're trying to win and you have a team that can act on what the tool finds, yes. If you're pre-revenue or you just want a dashboard to watch, start cheaper or start free. Pricing scales vary by growth stage, and there's a free AI-search assessment to test the claim before you talk to anyone.

Why AI search visibility is suddenly a real problem

The reason a category like this exists is simple: the traffic moved, and it's still moving.

Buyers stopped asking one search box and started asking five different assistants the same question, and now they’re getting five different answers about you. That's the shift. There's no longer a single surface to optimize for. If ChatGPT recommends your competitor while Perplexity doesn't mention you at all, you're losing deals you never see.

That's the job Goodie exists to do: see how you show up across all of those surfaces, and change it. Old SEO tools measure blue links, and they weren't built to tell you whether an AI engine recommends you or your competitor. Goodie exists to solve this new issue.

What Goodie is not

Goodie is not a keyword-rank tracker with an AI badge stuck on it, it's not a content generator that spits out articles and hopes, and it's not a set-and-forget button. It surfaces what's wrong and what to do, then someone on your side still has to ship the fix. I'd rather say that plainly than sell you a fantasy.

Key features

  • Visibility monitoring. Track brand share of voice, position, and mentions on up to 12 AI engines, refreshed daily.
  • Prompt and gap research. Find the actual prompts buyers use, and the topics where you're invisible while competitors get cited.

SKU-level product visibility across ChatGPT's agentic commerce, Google AI Mode Shopping, Amazon's Alexa for Shopping (formerly Rufus), and Perplexity Shopping, with feed remediation and schema injection.   • Goodie MCP. Pull Goodie's data into the tools you already work in.

What Goodie does well

  • It closes the loop. Monitoring is the easy part. Goodie's value is that it moves from "here's your score" to "here's the fix, ranked," and increasingly ships the fix. That's the whole design philosophy.
  • Breadth of coverage. Most tools track two or three engines. Watching up to twelve matters when your buyers are split across LLMs, and the split keeps shifting.
  • It reaches the digital shelf. The Agentic Commerce Suite is one of the few real answers to a question every ecommerce brand is about to ask: when someone shops through an AI agent, does it pick my product? Few competitors touch this yet.
  • Real outcomes, not vanity metrics. The reporting ties visibility to traffic and conversions, which is what earns the line item next budget cycle.
  • It flags misdescription, not just absence. Being cited isn't the same as being described right. Goodie tracks sentiment and share of voice against your competitors, so you catch an engine recommending you for the wrong thing, or getting a fact wrong, before it costs you a deal.
  • It sees the crawler side. Most tools stop at the answer. The Agent Experience Suite shows how AI crawlers and agents actually hit your site, then fixes the technical signals they read (llm.txt, sitemaps, structure) so you're not invisible for reasons you can't see.

Where Goodie falls short

A founder review that only lists strengths isn't a review, it's an ad. Here's the honest side.

  • It's priced for teams, not hobbyists. The entry point rules out a lot of early-stage users. If you're pre-revenue, this is more tool than you need today.
  • It's leverage, not magic. Goodie surfaces the fixes; someone still has to act on them. If nobody on your team has the capacity to execute, the insights just sit there.
  • Enterprise means a sales conversation. Core and Pro carry public prices; Enterprise and Agency Enterprise are quote-based. If you hate talking to sales before you see a number, that's friction at the top tier, and it's fair to call it that.
  • There's a learning curve. The platform is deep. Getting full value takes some onboarding, which is exactly why the higher tiers include a strategist.

Who Goodie AI is best for

It's a strong fit if you're a growth-stage or enterprise brand where AI search already drives discovery, an ecommerce brand worried about the agentic shelf, or an agency managing AI visibility for clients. In short: teams with traffic to defend and the capacity to act.

Look elsewhere if you're a solo founder or pre-revenue startup watching every dollar, or a team that wants a dashboard nobody has time to act on. Start with a free assessment and grow into a tool like this when the channel is real for you.

How Goodie compares

The honest way to place Goodie is by what you'd otherwise reach for. It falls into three buckets, and it's a different tool from all of them.

Goodie vs traditional SEO tools (Ahrefs, Semrush, and the like)

These are built for the blue-link world: keywords, backlinks, rank tracking on Google and Bing. They're indispensable for that job, and Goodie doesn't replace them. But they were never designed to tell you whether ChatGPT recommends you, how Perplexity describes you, or whether an AI Overview cites a competitor instead of you. That's a different signal on a different surface. The mental model I use: SEO tools tell you where you rank; Goodie tells you whether the machine that's now answering the question even mentions your name. Most teams should run both, and expect the overlap to shrink as more discovery moves into AI answers.

Goodie vs content generators and AI writers

A lot of what gets sold as "AI SEO" is really a content factory: type a keyword, get an article. Volume was never the problem. Publishing more pages that no model retrieves is just faster invisibility. Goodie's content layer works the other way around. It starts from the gap, the prompts you're losing and the topics where you're absent while others get cited, and drafts for retrieval rather than word count. It also doesn't stop at the draft, the job is to close a measured gap and then check whether the answer actually changed. A writer gives you words. Goodie is supposed to give you a reason to write them, and a way to know if they worked.

Goodie vs other AI visibility / GEO platforms

This is the direct-competitor category, and I'm obviously biased, so weigh it accordingly. The serious GEO platforms each have a real strength: some go deeper on enterprise demand data, others come in cheaper and built for agencies. Goodie's is breadth, up to 12 engines, and that it acts on what it finds instead of stopping at a score. If you're choosing between them, weigh three things: how many AI surfaces you actually need covered, whether you want a tool that only reports or one that also ships the fixes, and what you can spend. The verdict

If AI search is a channel you're trying to win and you have a team that can act on what the tool finds, Goodie earns its price. If you're pre-revenue or you just want something to watch, start cheaper or start free. I'd rather you use Goodie because it fits than because I talked you into it.

Where are you seeing your brand show up in AI answers right now? That's the question the whole category comes down to, and it's the one worth answering before you spend a dollar on any tool, mine included.
