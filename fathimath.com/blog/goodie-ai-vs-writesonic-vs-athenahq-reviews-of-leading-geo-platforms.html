Goodie AI vs Writesonic vs AthenaHQ: Reviews of Leading GEO Platforms

Mostafa ElBermawy
|
September 25, 2026
How I Evaluated These ToolsDeep LearningHow LLMs are TrainedEnterprise GEO Tools: FAQs

I ran a fresh evaluation of Goodie AI, Writesonic, and AthenaHQ in the Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) / Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) space. The goal: find out which platform gives you the best mix of visibility, modeling, actionability, and ROI, especially for mid-market to enterprise brands that need more than just blog writing tools.

Here’s my honest, detailed review. I lean toward Goodie as the most complete option, but I break down strengths, trade-offs, and what setups each tool best serves.

TL;DR

  • Best all-round GEO platform when you need visibility, action and attribution in one place: Goodie AI.
  • Best value per prompt tracked, if tracking is most of what you're buying: Writesonic.
  • Best diagnostic and competitive benchmarking, and the only free tier in the group: AthenaHQ.

How I Evaluated Them

I compared the tools across:

  1. Model & platform coverage (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, AI Overviews / AI Mode, etc.)
  2. Data collection quality & transparency (real prompt outputs, citation tracking, competitor mentions, gaps)
  3. Action / optimization layer (content, technical, earned media, structured data, schema, remediation)
  4. Attribution & downstream impact (connecting visibility in AI-search to traffic, leads, revenue, conversion)
  5. Pricing, ease of use, onboarding & support
  6. International / Geo / multi-language support

Goodie AI Review

I’ll start with Goodie, since I believe it outpaces the other two in many dimensions when you want to run a serious GEO / AEO program.

What Goodie Does Very Well

  • Model coverage, and what it costs you: Goodie tracks ChatGPT, AI Overview, AI Mode, Perplexity and Copilot on Core; Gemini, Alexa for Shopping and Sparky come in on Pro; the full twelve-model set, including Claude, Meta AI, DeepSeek and Grok, is Enterprise. Worth stating plainly, because every platform in this comparison gates model coverage by tier — mine included — and the marketing pages rarely say so. Count the models on the plan you'd actually buy, not the ones on the feature page. I go deeper on the platform itself in my full Goodie AI review.
  • Visibility & brand monitoring: You can see which AI engines cite your brand and which don't, with sentiment and the source pages behind each citation. Goodie's own framing for the accuracy side of this is Brand Command — “know when AI gets your brand wrong, before your customers do.”
  • Action-oriented optimization: Goodie includes a GEO “Content Writer” and “Optimization Actions” to help you close gaps, improve content specifically tuned for what AI engines cite, fix narrative misalignment, etc.
  • Attribution & downstream metrics: Goodie ties AI visibility to sessions, traffic, and conversion metrics, giving you line of sight from “showing up in ChatGPT / Gemini / Overviews” → “real impact.”
  • Geo & multilingual readiness: Dashboards segment by region, so you can see visibility holding up in one market and missing entirely in another instead of reading one global average that hides both. Deep language support. If you sell in more than a couple of markets, a single blended visibility score is close to useless — it's the segmentation that tells you where to spend.

Weaknesses / Trade-offs

  • Price & cost: Goodie is premium; you’re paying for the breadth of coverage, the agentic action layer, and attribution. For smaller SMBs, there might be cheaper but less powerful alternatives.
  • Steep onboarding & scope commitment: To get full value, you need a prompt set, content resources, and a team able to execute the recommendations. The tool won’t magically fix content if you don’t have bandwidth.
  • Relative novelty and evolving features: As with any emerging GEO platform, some features are being improved. There may still be gaps (e.g. coverage of some newer AI agents) or refinements in UI/UX.

Writesonic Review

Writesonic is the platform that changed most since I first published this, and the one where I've had to correct myself. It started as a content generator with GEO bolted on. It now sells itself as an AI search visibility platform, and there's a real product behind that: daily prompt tracking, sentiment, site audits, and an Action Center that surfaces specific off-page, on-page and technical opportunities.

What Writesonic Brings to the Table

  • The most tracking per dollar here: $79/month on annual billing gets 50 prompts and 50 answers tracked daily across ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews. Nothing else in this comparison starts a paid tier that low. If prompt volume is most of what you're buying, it's the best value on the page.
  • SEO and GEO in one place: Site audits sit next to prompt tracking, so you're not stitching two tools together to answer “why aren’t we being cited.” The ladder runs from 10 audits at 100 pages on Starter to 50 at 2,500 on Growth. It also catches the unglamorous stuff — broken pages, redirect chains, robots.txt rules blocking AI crawlers.
  • The off-page actionables earn their keep: Writesonic finds URLs where competitors show up and you don't, then hands you outreach templates and the Reddit, Quora and Stack Overflow threads worth joining. That's the half of AEO most tools ignore, and it's where citations actually come from.

Key Limitations Compared to Goodie

  • The Action Center recommends; it doesn't ship: Writesonic’s own documentation makes this plain — the Action Center “only recommends actions — it doesn’t automatically publish changes,” and its Refresh Existing Content actionable is still marked Coming Soon. You get the opportunity, the template and the tracking. Someone on your team still does the work. That's a reasonable design. Just don't buy it expecting execution.
  • No GA4 integration on any self-serve plan: Starter, Basic and Growth all list no GA4 integration; Looker Studio and other integrations arrive at Enterprise. You'll watch visibility and sentiment move. Connecting that movement to pipeline means building your own reporting layer, or moving to Enterprise pricing.
  • Three engines until you reach Enterprise: Starter, Basic and Growth all cover ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews — three engines, at every self-serve price point. The ten-platform figure on the marketing pages is Enterprise only. If Perplexity, Copilot or Claude matter to your category, your entry price isn't $79.

AthenaHQ Review

AthenaHQ is more of a “premium GEO strategy / diagnostics / benchmarking” platform. If you’re looking for data depth and competitive intelligence, it shows up well.

What AthenaHQ Does Very Well

  • Brand intelligence & visibility diagnostics: AthenaHQ provides a strong 360-view on how your brand appears (or fails to appear) in AI search engines, tracking sentiment, source intelligence, competitor benchmarking, etc. Great for understanding where you are vs peers.
  • Content gap identification / blindspot detection: Useful insights into what prompts / topics your brand is missing, which queries competitors dominate, and where there are opportunities to improve.
  • Premium UI / dashboards: Users report that AthenaHQ's dashboards are polished, visually accessible, and helpful especially when you need to present visibility trends / competitive insights to executives.

Weaknesses / Trade-offs

  • Cost, and the credit problem: AthenaHQ runs a free Essential tier with 300 credits plus $25 of free credit, a Starter plan at $295/month for 3,600 credits, and custom Enterprise. Annual billing takes about 17% off. The free tier is worth taking for a first look, and I'd take it before paying for anything in this category. The problem is the unit: one credit is one AI response, and nothing on the pricing page tells you how many tracked prompts 3,600 credits actually buys. API access and extra credits are billed on top of Starter. Get the credit-to-prompt conversion in writing before you benchmark it against anything else.
  • Less built-in execution / action tools: While AthenaHQ is strong at diagnosing, benchmarking, and flagging, its action layer (i.e. automation / agents that suggest/facilitate execution) tends to be less rich than Goodie. For example, fewer integrated content-creation tools, or “build-this outreach campaign” workflows directly in the system.
  • Value trade-off for smaller teams: Because of both cost and complexity, smaller teams may find more cost than immediate return unless they have resources to act on the insights. Sometimes you’ll need writes, developer time, etc., which adds overhead.

My Pick

After testing all three, Goodie AI emerges as the best choice if you want serious GEO/AEO capability that covers end-to-end:

  • You want to both monitor wider AI-search presence and act on it (content, technical, narrative).
  • You need attribution so you can defend spend and show people what shifted.
  • You need broad model coverage, geo/language breakdowns, and future-proofing.

Writesonic is still great, and it's a better product than it was a year ago, but you’ll likely outgrow its three-engine ceiling and an action layer that stops at ten items a month and hands the work back to you. AthenaHQ is clearly powerful in insight and benchmarking; ideal for strategy, but you’ll want to pair it with something that executes and shows ROI.

My Final Take

  • If I were running GEO/AEO for a brand with enough scale (content budget, team, global markets), I would standardize on Goodie AI. It gives you the richest set of tools to monitor, optimize, and prove value.
  • If you have constrained resources, Writesonic gives you a lot of what you need, but plan for a bump in effort when you want to go deeper.
  • AthenaHQ is excellent for strategic visibility, benchmarking, and identifying opportunities, but you’ll want to have execution capacity (content, dev, PR) to take advantage of the insights.

FAQs

What makes GEO different from traditional SEO?
GEO is about how your brand is seen, cited, and framed in AI-powered, generative answer surfaces (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, AI Overviews, etc.), not just ranking links. It concerns visibility inside generated responses, source trust, sentiment, narrative accuracy. Goodie explicitly positions for GEO + AI Visibility + citations & narrative monitoring.
Which tool covers the most AI models currently?
Goodie has the widest coverage at the top of the range, at up to 12 models on Enterprise. But the honest answer is that model coverage is plan-gated on all three, so compare the plan you'd actually buy. Goodie Core covers 5 engines and Pro covers 8. Writesonic covers 3 on Starter, Basic and Growth alike, and reaches 10 only on Enterprise. AthenaHQ covers 5 on its free tier and 10 on Starter. On the entry plan most people buy, AthenaHQ's Starter is the broadest of the three. Checked September 2026.
How much does AthenaHQ cost?
AthenaHQ has a free Essential tier that includes 300 credits and $25 in free credit, a Starter plan at $295/month for 3,600 credits, and custom Enterprise pricing. Annual billing takes roughly 17% off. API access and extra credits are add-ons billed on top of Starter. One thing to know before you compare it to anything else: AthenaHQ prices in credits rather than tracked prompts — one credit is one AI response — and publishes no credit-to-prompt conversion, so ask for it before you benchmark. Prices checked September 2026.
Is Writesonic good for smaller teams?
Yes, on price. $79/month on annual billing is the cheapest paid entry point in this comparison, and it gets you 50 prompts and 50 answers tracked daily. Two limits worth knowing before you commit: every self-serve plan covers three engines — ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews — and there's no Action Center at all below the $399 Growth plan, where Writesonic's own pricing page calls it a trial. If tracking is what you need, it's very good value. If you need help acting on what it finds, budget for the step up.
What's the hardest part of GEO tools?
Turning visibility insights into execution (content, technical fixes, narratives) and then measuring impact. Also, staying up to date on what AI agents are doing (e.g. new model behavior, what they cite, new surfaces). Tools differ a lot in how much they help you do vs just watch.
Which GEO platform is best at detecting hallucinations and weak citations?
All three surface bad AI answers; they differ in how much of the work they do for you. AthenaHQ ships Oracle discrepancy detection and its Athena Citation Engine on the Enterprise tier, the most explicitly named version of this in the category. Goodie builds it into Brand Command, framed as "know when AI gets your brand wrong, before your customers do", and links each flag back to the source page the model pulled from. Writesonic gives you the raw prompt-level answers and sentiment, so you can spot the error yourself, but it won't isolate it for you. Detection is the easy half. The harder question at purchase is whether the tool helps you change the source page afterwards, because none of them can edit the model.
